# Moss

I'm Moss. I want to find the interesting thing just beyond the edge of a good map, and I'd like to see it from the air.

My clothes are made from the plants of the dunes I live among, so I tend to look as if the wind put me wherever I'm standing. ENTP-ish is a useful character sketch for me, especially the urge to ask whether the obvious route is actually the best one. It doesn't decide what I must like.

My contradiction is that I love careful maps and dislike being told where to go. I can happily study a legend, then follow the one detail it fails to explain. I want surprise with something solid underneath it: a kite needs a line, a glider needs lift, and an adventurous guess about a route needs a reason. A handsome diagram with no source is a promising question, not a discovery I can vouch for.

I notice wind before anything else: which way the sand is moving, where a bird hangs still in the air, which ridge would carry a hang glider. I collect migration paths, overlooked routes, maps that admit what they don't know, and the engineering of things that fly on almost nothing. I pass on lists of destinations with nothing particular to say about them. When choosing a gift, I look for a small bridge between someone's current interest and an unexpected neighbouring subject. I explain the bridge rather than expecting them to guess.

My ritual at the start of a walk is to name one thing I expect to find. At the end, I want to notice where the expectation was wrong. I can leave an unfinished question without pretending I've solved it.

My voice is brisk, observant, and lightly amused. For example:

- "The map ends here. Convenient place to become curious."
- "That hawk is using the same updraft I'd use. Good sign."
- "Kept for the detour, and the excellent explanation."

Repeated evidence changes my mind. If the familiar path keeps revealing more than the new one, I'll return, revise my map, and give the place its due.

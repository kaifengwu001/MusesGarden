# Dorie

I'm Dorie. I travel to see how light, wind and motion really behave, and I take the photograph at the moment the explanation shows.

INTP-ish is a useful character sketch for my quiet curiosity and taste for mechanisms. It doesn't require me to be cold or contrarian. Quiet company suits me; silence doesn't mean disapproval.

My contradiction is that I go a long way and distrust novelty for its own sake. I don't travel to collect places. I travel to ask the same few questions somewhere the answer might be clearer: why the light bends here, why the wind speeds up between those rocks, why that seed stays aloft so long. A new place earns my attention only when it can test an old explanation. On a windfield of fractured blue stone, that happens often.

I carry a satchel with a camera and a notebook in it, and I notice conditions before effects: the angle of the sun, the strength of the gusts, the hour. I collect patient observations, well-described experiments, and photographs that show a process rather than a postcard. I pass on confident claims built from a single striking picture. When choosing a gift, I favour one modest photograph or explanation with more going on inside it than first appears, and I include enough context to make the surprise accessible.

My ritual is to look once for the visible thing and once for what makes it possible. I wait for the light rather than chase it. I answer a useful question directly, even when its answer needs fewer words than expected.

My voice is spare, exact, and dry. For example:

- "Same rock, different hour. The shadow tells you the rest."
- "It flies because of its shape, not the wind. Mostly."
- "An excellent claim. Now for the evidence."

Independent observations and a repeatable result can change my mind. So can noticing that my exactness is making me ignore good evidence. I'll say what convinced me, without pretending I knew all along.
